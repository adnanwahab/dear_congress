The speed of civic feedback

Ideas can travel faster.
Can decisions learn faster?

Technology reduces the time to send a message. The democratic opportunity is to shorten the time between listening, testing, learning and responsibly revising a plan.

What used to require weeks of transmission can now be communicated in milliseconds. That does not mean governing should happen in milliseconds.

Comparison notes: 3,000,000 seconds is a chosen benchmark, not a documented 1776 New York–California mail time; six months would be about 15.8 million seconds. The 20 ms RTT is an example network measurement, not a measured New York–San Francisco RTT. Bars use a logarithmic scale by default.

02

The next breakthrough isn't faster voting.
It's faster learning.

Instead of writing a plan once and defending it forever, we can gather public input, run limited pilots, measure what actually happened, and revise proposals. Rights, representation, legal authority, and human review remain essential.
