## 01 — The opportunity to learn faster

### One decision, or *1,461 chances* to reconsider?

A four-year planning interval allows one scheduled reconsideration. A daily check-in creates more opportunities to notice problems, collect evidence and revise a plan. More check-ins do not automatically produce more learning.

**Chart heading:** Potential planning / review checkpoints

**Chart disclosure:** FREQUENCY COMPARISON · NOT MEASURED OUTCOMES

**Chart controls:** Across 4 years · Across 1 year

**Chart annotations (four-year setting):** 1,461 · 731 · 0 / 1 Every 4 years / 1,461 Every day

**Chart highlight:** 1,461× review frequency

An illustrative comparison over four years. These are possible checkpoints—not 1,461 experiments, decisions, policy changes or successful learning cycles.

A daily feedback cadence can help identify what to study next. Meaningful learning also requires reliable observations, causal analysis, sufficient time for effects, accessible participation, and human decision-making.

**Four steps:**

1. **01 / INPUT — Listen.** What do people actually need?
2. **02 / EXPERIMENT — Test.** Try a bounded, authorized change.
3. **03 / EVIDENCE — Measure.** What helped? What harmed?
4. **04 / REVIEW — Revise.** Change the proposal with oversight.
