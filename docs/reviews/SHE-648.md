## Behavior

A shared opt-in dialog appears in BillCatalog and WorkflowScreen. During the
guided demo its launcher lives in DemoTour instead, and opening pauses the tour.
Tour keyboard shortcuts yield to puzzle controls; Escape closes the puzzle
without exiting the tour. The browser's modal dialog contains focus, each scene
focuses its heading, and Exit returns focus to the launcher. Native buttons and
an expandable text explanation provide keyboard and touch targets. Shared theme
tokens, chip styling and source provenance styling are reused.

All scenes identify Lantern City as invented. Players recover building,
electrical and mechanical permits, resolve an obsolete-memo/checklist conflict,
and order work, inspection and occupancy review before a conditional fictional
Friday opening. Wrong answers explain the dependency without a penalty. No
countdown, ideological scores, rewards or participation privileges exist. Skip,
Restart, Exit and a complete text solution are available at every scene. Closing
and reopening begins again. State is component memory only: no storage, request,
ballot, proposal, receipt or official record API is used by the puzzle.

The City of Houston business portal's [Permits and Inspections](https://www.houstontx.gov/business/plan/permits-inspections.html)
was inspected through web search on November 15, 2026. It supports the limited
explanation distinguishing construction/trade permits, inspection and commercial
occupancy authorization. The fictional three-permit scenario, scheduling conflict
and timeline are explicitly not sourced real requirements or turnaround claims.
The app links the original publisher and explains that scope. This is an
educational illustration, not a project-specific permitting guide.
