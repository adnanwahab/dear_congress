## Implemented slice

The exact fictional demo-transit v1 editorial/source snapshot now offers a fixed,
roughly two-minute English script with four cited chapters, comparable reasons
and concerns, editorial attribution, synthetic-voice disclosure and visible
transcript. Browser speech reads the same strings verbatim; no generative model,
new API, upload, audio file, persistence, or supporting evidence is introduced.
Browser/device speech processing is disclosed before playback. Actual duration
varies by voice and selected speed (0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5).

Playback is explicit and offers pause/resume, stop, chapter restart, loading,
error/retry and unsupported-browser states. Speed changes stop playback and
require another play action. A twelve-second startup timeout recovers a stalled
voice. Cancellation invalidates old speech callbacks. Revision removal, unmount
and pagehide stop the briefing; competing briefing instances share one owner.
Unrelated active speech is not canceled. Transcript access never requires audio.
Controls have native keyboard semantics, visible focus and 44px minimum targets;
layout wraps without animation, including reduced-motion mode.

Source, editorial or proposal changes withhold the briefing. Namespace checks
keep citizen/repository items out; advisory count changes do not invalidate it.
The source snapshot is pinned independently of future catalog edits. Existing
stage navigation, votes, repository/item-kind identities, PR head/base bindings,
records and receipts are unchanged. Public help now explains the feature and
its trust boundaries.

Citations refer to the fictional clause and existing editorial arguments. METRO
is expressly an agency background homepage with unknown freshness, not claim
support or agency endorsement. Both sides retain their missing-evidence limits.
This is a demo implementation, not a sourced real-policy briefing or proof of
verified participation. Human evidence/editorial review is still required for
real-policy use; no fictitious balancing evidence was added.
