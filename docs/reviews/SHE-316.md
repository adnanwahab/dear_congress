## Bounded prototype proposal (not an approved protocol)

First define a narrow advisory eligibility policy, jurisdiction and expiry window, with legal/privacy review of whether age/residency checks are appropriate. Provider selection must compare data collection, duplicate enrollment controls, exclusion/error rates, accessibility, revocation, retention, subprocessors and breach/collusion risk. No vendor or cryptographic scheme is selected here.

Use a separately operated eligibility issuer and ballot verifier. Investigate independently reviewed anonymous credentials or blind issuance that can prove the required predicate without sending identity or issuer subject IDs to the ballot service. A separate database alone does not prevent joins through logs, timestamps, networks, cookies or provider collusion. An ordinary signed JWT containing a stable subject, or a hash of an email/address, fails this objective.

Issuer-side records should contain only the minimum revocable eligibility status, policy version, expiry and protected anti-duplication material necessary for that policy. Do not ingest identity documents, full birth dates, names or addresses into Dear Congress. A reviewed protocol must explain duplicate enrollment across providers, reissuance/device recovery, key rotation and revocation without giving the issuer a list of ballot nullifiers or choices.

The ballot verifier should receive an unlinkable eligibility proof and a domain-separated anti-replay/nullifier value scoped to the exact civic proposal/revision, jurisdiction, policy and voting window. Repository ballots, if ever considered separately, must retain exact repository ID, item kind and PR head/base bindings. A global participant token is unacceptable. Consume the proof atomically with ballot acceptance; retrying an identical request must not add a second count. Never trust a client-supplied verified flag. Concurrent writes and restart recovery need transactional storage, not an unreviewed extension of the JSON writer.

Credential records must never share browser IDs, account/session identifiers or public aliases with ballot storage. A ballot nullifier may itself link revisions/choices within its intended scope: minimize this scope and access, never publish it, and assess it explicitly during external review. Do not claim even this conceptual separation proves secrecy. Timing correlation, application compromise and issuer/verifier collusion remain prototype rejection criteria until evaluated.

Specify withdrawal and eligibility revocation separately. Revocation should block new proofs promptly; whether an already accepted anonymous ballot remains in a closed tally requires an explicit published policy. Removing a person's old ballot by issuer identity conflicts with unlinkability. Do not promise both without a reviewed mechanism. Define retention periods and backup expiry before collecting real claims; provide receipt-based ballot controls only if they do not create transferable proof of choice.

## User experience contract

At `/votes`, show distinct labeled sections: **Verified eligible participants — advisory**, **Unverified browser votes — advisory**, and **Synthetic demo data — illustrative**. Before rollout, verified status is “Not available”; never infer zero verified participants from a missing service or fabricate badges. Explain each count's denominator and eligibility scope/expiry. Do not sum these groups into “people” or official support; keep synthesis priors explicitly illustrative.

At a future `/account`, explain optional verification, provider disclosure, collected predicates, retention, expiry and limits before redirect. Offer a keyboard-accessible “Continue anonymously” path with equal advisory access. Show private pending/eligible/expired/revoked/unavailable states, renewal, revocation and support options. Public profiles must not expose eligibility linked to choices. Failure or refusal to verify must not silently promote a browser ballot or remove anonymous participation.

Do not silently move or duplicate an old advisory vote when verifying. For an initial pilot, keep modes separate, do not report a deduplicated cross-track people total, and explain that a person may appear in both tracks. Any later transition needs explicit participant action and privacy review; retaining a hidden identity-to-old-choice mapping defeats the objective.

## Threat model and prototype gates

| Threat | Required control and residual risk to review |
| --- | --- |
| Sybils | Issuer enrollment assurance, cross-provider duplicate policy and scoped nullifiers; cookie/IP limits alone fail. Stolen, shared and purchased credentials remain risks. |
| Coercion | No public individual choices, identity-linked history or transferable choice receipt. Screen observation/device control cannot be eliminated; never claim coercion resistance without specialist review. |
| Replay | Audience/policy/revision/window binding, proof freshness, atomic consumption and idempotent retry; test cross-scope replay, concurrent submissions, crash and restore. |
| Data breach | Separate trust boundaries/keys, least privilege, encrypted storage/backups, log redaction, short retention and tested deletion. Assess combined issuer, ballot, access-log and backup compromise; hashing is insufficient. |
| Correlation | Avoid common analytics/session IDs and precise public timestamps; evaluate batching and delayed release. Small-cohort/differencing attacks require suppression and controls on repeated queries. |
| Provider compromise/outage | Fail closed for verified counts, keep anonymous advisory access; demonstrate revocation/key rotation and reissuance cannot mint duplicate counts. |
| Exclusion and misrepresentation | Measure false rejection and accessibility barriers, provide appeal/support, label self-selection and scope; no population mandate or official authority claims. |

## Delivery and acceptance plan

1. Post-launch discovery: confirm SHE-276/SHE-279 acceptance on the recovered baseline, establish source/release provenance with SHE-281, agree eligibility and retention policy, and commission a provider/protocol feasibility spike using synthetic identities only.
2. Prototype: isolate issuer/verifier stores and logs; demonstrate issuance, expiry, revocation, concurrency, recovery and independent aggregation. Stop if provider or protocol requires stable identity-to-choice linkage. Do not collect real credentials merely to validate UI.
3. Tests: positive valid proof counts once in the verified section; anonymous participation persists only in its section; sample data remains illustrative. Negative tests reject forged/wrong issuer, expired/revoked, wrong jurisdiction/audience/policy/revision, duplicate and replayed proofs, client verified flags and cross-repository/item-kind replay. Test retries, restarts and concurrent consumption with disposable storage; preserve existing votes, source IDs and receipts.
4. Privacy/provenance review: trace every public endpoint, export, aggregate, analytics event, log and backup for credential/choice joins; scan public outputs for seeded canary identities, subject IDs and nullifiers. Review dependency/provider provenance and key custody. Aggregate-only output and suppressed small cohorts must resist repeated differencing; test this rather than asserting anonymity.
5. Mobile/accessibility: check `/votes` and `/account` at 320/390px and zoom; keyboard navigation, labels/status announcements, focus restoration after provider return, error/retry and expired consent; screen-reader review and human comprehension testing of all three participation labels.
6. Before consequential use: obtain independent external security/privacy review, resolve findings and explicitly approve a narrowly scoped opt-in pilot. Record review scope/revision and limits without publishing sensitive findings. The rough 4–10 week estimate requires spike validation. No acceptance criterion is complete based on this report.
