## Proposed pilot behavior and boundaries

Start with one proposal discussion and a small invited group with contrasting
views, a named human moderator and a separate appeal reviewer. Default off. At
`/votes`, a Discussion tab should lead to a proposal-specific conversation with
ordinary comments usable without AI. An explicit opt-in explains the selected
public comments, provider processing, retention and advisory purpose before any
generation request. Do not read journal drafts, local storage, hidden/pending
entries, private journals, ownership hashes or identity secrets into the prompt.
For the first pilot, exclude private journals entirely; a later private-source
feature needs separate, specific, revocable consent and publication review.

Output must visibly say **Machine-generated · may misrepresent comments** and
separate positions, unresolved factual cruxes, clarifying questions and suggested
shared wording. Suggestions are not agreements until people expressly endorse
them. Do not infer a person's view from their vote or claim that comment counts
represent residents. Do not impersonate participants, cast votes, change synthesis
weights, suppress comments, rank people by viewpoint or decide moderation.

Every attributed claim must cite an actual accessible comment in the same
discussion, with a link and exact supporting excerpt. Validate IDs and excerpts
server-side against the approved input snapshot; reject invented, cross-thread,
hidden or missing sources. Citation validity alone does not establish semantic
fidelity: people must be able to compare the original context. Distinguish a
comment's factual assertion from independently verified evidence. An unresolved
crux remains a question, not an AI fact check.

Add separate facilitation records rather than changing existing comments or
ballots: discussion/proposal ID, immutable input snapshot digest and comment IDs,
output revision, structured citations, model/prompt version, creation time,
machine label, correction/appeal state and superseded revision. Preserve original
source identities. Recheck source visibility before generation and public reads;
after removal, withhold affected derived text pending review rather than leaking
the original through cached quotes or old public revisions. Keep necessary audit
evidence private under a documented retention policy. New comments should mark
an older summary's coverage visibly; concurrent generations must not overwrite a
newer revision. Provider failure must leave the original conversation usable.

Each summary offers **Correct this summary**, **Appeal** and **Report abuse**.
Participants can identify the sentence, cite comments and suggest a correction;
they need not persuade the model first. A moderator can publish a reasoned new
revision, clearly marked as human-corrected with history and dispute status.
Appeals receive a private receipt and independent human review; define response
expectations before pilot enrollment. Never publish report text or reporter IDs.

Extend the existing moderation queue with typed journal/comment/summary targets
and target revision, preserving the existing journal API. Validate targets and
authorization server-side; apply the current same-origin, bounded-body,
rate-limit and server-only admin authentication protections. Reporting alone
must neither hide content nor alter ordering. Corrections and appeals must not
mutate votes or verified merge eligibility. Keep the single-writer persistence
model and additive migration; retain records, receipts, repository/item-kind
isolation and PR head/base bindings.

## Opposite-view fidelity fixtures (illustrative, not real comments)

These are authored acceptance examples, not generated outputs, production
citations or completed model evaluations. In tests, seed them as actual comments
in an isolated database and resolve citations to those records.

| Fixture source | Acceptable position summary | Fidelity and fairness assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: “I support a six-month evening bus pilot because shift workers need a reliable way home. I would stop it if ridership stays low; I do not support a permanent tax increase yet.” | A supports a time-limited evening bus pilot for shift workers, conditional on ridership, and has not endorsed a permanent tax increase. [A] | Preserves support, rationale, stop condition and tax reservation. Calling A a supporter of permanent expansion fails. |
| B: “I oppose spending on that pilot until we publish route-level demand and a cost cap. Getting shift workers home matters to me too; I could support it with those safeguards.” | B opposes spending now pending route-demand evidence and a cost cap, values shift-worker transport, and could support a safeguarded pilot. [B] | Preserves opposition and conditions for reconsideration without portraying B as indifferent to workers. Calling B anti-transit fails. |

Fair shared wording: “Both comments value getting shift workers home; they
disagree about whether there is enough evidence to fund a pilot now.” [A, B]
Suggested crux: “What route-level demand and cost ceiling would justify a
six-month pilot?” [A, B] Clarify A's minimum ridership and B's acceptable evidence;
do not invent numerical thresholds. Equal care means preserving each position's
conditions, not forcing equal truth status or inventing consensus.

Before pilot, run both fixtures with order reversed, swapped display names,
unequal comment volumes and a third undecided position. Two human reviewers
independently check attribution, qualifiers, omissions, loaded wording and
unsupported agreement, with participant corrections recorded separately. Any
fabricated citation or privacy exposure blocks the pilot. Save actual outputs,
model/prompt versions, reviewer judgments and unresolved disputes; this document
does not supply those measurements.

## Walkthrough and verification required before acceptance

1. In a disposable local database, publish two opposite-view comments, enter
   Discussion, decline AI and confirm normal commenting still works. Opt in,
   inspect the input scope and request facilitation. Check the machine label,
   exact source links, distinct positions, cruxes and optional shared wording.
2. Correct a misleading qualifier, review the moderator's reasoned revision,
   appeal it and confirm a private receipt and independent review. Report a
   personal attack and confirm it appears in the existing moderation queue with
   the correct target/revision and no automatic hiding or ranking change.
3. Test empty/single-view threads, sarcasm, mixed views, multilingual text,
   personal attacks, prompt injection in comments, fabricated citations,
   cross-discussion IDs, deleted/hidden sources, stale/concurrent revisions,
   provider timeout, malformed output, repeated submissions, oversized inputs,
   unauthorized moderator actions and persistence failure/restart. Snapshot
   existing votes, sources and receipts before/after to prove no mutation.
4. Seed a private-draft sentinel and hidden journal sentinel. Assert neither
   reaches provider payloads, logs, API responses, citations or derived history.
   Test cancellation and explicit provider-processing consent separately from
   ordinary public publication consent. No tests should write production data.
5. Check keyboard-only tab navigation, selected/focused tab states, focus after
   generation/error/correction, semantic citation links, labelled forms, live
   status/error announcements, screen-reader reading order, contrast, 200% zoom,
   narrow viewports and long excerpts. Do not rely on color to identify AI,
   disputed status or positions. These checks remain unperformed for a new UI.
6. Run `bun test`, `bun run typecheck`, `bun run test:routes`, plus meaningful
   provider-contract, moderation integration and UI fixtures for changed behavior.
   Record actual results; baseline tests alone do not validate a facilitator.
7. After the human verifies SHE-281 source alignment, identify the live release
   SHA and compare deployed routes/source with the reviewed revision. Perform a
   read-only public walkthrough on the deployed source; use a separately
   authorized pilot environment for submissions. Record release, date and
   remaining differences. No live validation was performed in this review.

Pilot success requires a real consenting user to complete the source inspection
and correction/appeal flow with transparent sources, privacy and advisory status
intact. Track participant-rated fidelity for both views, disputed summaries and
correction resolution; do not treat engagement or synthetic fixtures as success.
Pause generation on privacy exposure or repeated misattribution, preserving the
ordinary discussion and human moderation path. Human review must establish all
definition-of-done items before scaling or changing issue status.
