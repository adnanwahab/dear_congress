# SHE-897 — sample reading clarity

Refs: https://linear.app/shel-bernstein/issue/SHE-897/cs-l100050-edit-all-12-sample-summaries-for-8th-grade-reading-clarity

## Scope and audit

Reviewed repository README, launch merge policy, federal metadata documentation,
DemoItem fixtures, provenance UI and workflow rendering. GitHub PR search for
SHE-897 returned no matches; open PRs included separate simulation/handoff work
(SHE-899/901/902/903). This change does not implement that work.

All 12 fictional samples now have two plain-language sentences and a specific key
caveat. Feed cards, selected Feed detail, Source desk and Votes use the shared
read-only explanation. Native details/summary retains the exact illustrative
current approach and proposed text, with an individually named expansion.
No official legal claims were researched or added. Background links, publishers,
source IDs, proposed text, current text, sponsors and seeded counts are unchanged.
The wording distinguishes targets from measurements or modeled outcomes. The food
sample explicitly separates discounted delivery from the narrower draft requirement.
Mental health's two-week target is not presented as a guarantee.
The transit audio briefing remains pinned to its historical editorial revision;
the new summary deliberately shows the unavailable-briefing message until a
separate script review. Tests retain historical playback coverage and assert
that the new summary cannot silently reuse that briefing.

## Human acceptance still pending

This is an editorial candidate, not proof of eighth-grade comprehension or legal
review. No comprehension readers participated; no reader results are fabricated.
Leave the issue open until a human verifies acceptance. Before rollout, an editor
and source/safety reviewers must compare every summary and caveat to the retained
draft wording. Check native keyboard expansion, screen reader announcement,
390px layout and 200% zoom in a browser.

Use consenting readers comfortable with roughly eighth-grade material. Present
each sample with its caveat, without coaching, then ask:

1. What would this idea change, and who or what does it cover?
2. What does it leave uncertain or not promise?
3. Is this a real bill or law? Does an advisory vote change the law?
4. Open Full details. Where can you find the draft wording, and does it change
   your understanding?

For each of the 12 IDs, record the reviewed revision, reader count, anonymized
paraphrases, misunderstandings, expansion usability and editorial corrections.
Require accurate scope, key caveat and fictional/non-binding recognition before
accepting each sample; revise and repeat any misunderstood item. Reader identity
and sensitive personal information need not be recorded. Automated sentence and
render tests cannot substitute for this review.
