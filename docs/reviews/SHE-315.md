## Source provenance review and integration risks

These are code-level findings; provider schemas and retrieved records still need
independent review before integration approval.

- FEMA: queries NFIP claims for county code 48201, counts loss years and computes
  a three-year average. Insured claims omit uninsured losses; claim revisions,
  coverage uptake and storm intensity can change counts independently of policy.
  Missing years are omitted, and the average uses available years, so it must not
  silently be presented as a complete three-year window. Archive query, pagination
  evidence, extraction date, raw snapshot and transformation version.
- Census: fetches ACS one-year B08303 total and two long-commute cells for Harris
  County, starting in 2015. This is a share of workers with long commutes, not a
  direct measure of bus service quality or all residents. Non-OK yearly responses
  are silently skipped if other years succeed; missing years need explicit status.
  Verify headers/variables, missing-value codes, release comparability, margins of
  error, population denominator and publication lag before approving a series.
- Houston 311: combines recent and archived ArcGIS feeds, filters street-like
  types and takes median closure time by creation year. No deduplication is visible;
  overlap, schema changes, open-case exclusion and administrative closure practices
  can bias trends. Closure is an operational metric, not direct evidence of safer
  streets. Validate stable IDs, pagination completeness and right censoring.
- All adapters need full schema validation, safe handling of malformed/partial
  responses and publication/revision dates distinct from retrieval time. Existing
  source caveats are useful but do not substitute for reproducible provenance.

## Proposed post-launch spike

Time-box an initial prototype to about one week before committing the proposed
3–6 week build. A maintainer and data steward choose one documented implemented
policy, its responsible jurisdiction and at least two measurable promises/tradeoffs.
If no defensible baseline or usable source exists, the prototype must show
“cannot yet be measured” rather than invent data. Use synthetic fixtures explicitly
labeled as such for failure-state demonstrations, and separately archived,
reviewed public data for any real-policy example.

Proposed records, separate from existing journals, votes and tracker storage:

- **Policy revision:** stable jurisdiction/source identifier, policy version,
  official enactment and implementation records/dates, scope and promise citations
  with page/section, author of registration, review history and supersession link.
  A fictional demo ID or repository PR merge is not enactment evidence.
- **Metric contract revision:** policy reference, metric definition, unit,
  geography/population/denominator, desired direction, baseline period/value and
  pinned observation version, target value/date, data owner, update cadence,
  expected publication lag, freshness rule, uncertainty method and caveats.
  Preserve original promises when targets are revised; record who changed them
  and why. Do not silently rebase history.
- **Observation/source revision:** observation period, value or explicit missing
  reason, uncertainty bounds, release and retrieval dates, exact source query and
  dataset release, content hash plus retained snapshot location, transform version,
  reviewer, quality flags and superseded revision. Use an append-only correction
  history and reproducible derived values; never rely solely on a mutable URL.
- **Assessment:** references to the exact policy, contract and observations used,
  as-of date, comparable/not-comparable reason, descriptive movement, target status,
  freshness and evidence limitations. Store causal evidence separately, defaulting
  to “not established.” A reviewed comparison design would need its own methods,
  comparator, assumptions, confounders, uncertainty and limitations.

Compute movement only across comparable units, populations and periods. Separate
“improved/worsened/no clear change/uncertain” from “target met/not met/not yet due”
and from “current/stale/missing/not comparable.” A favorable estimate can remain
uncertain; an unmet future target is not automatically a failure. Register
subgroup harms where defensible aggregate data supports them, with disclosure
controls for small populations. Never collapse mixed metrics into one success score.

For example, a **synthetic** transit prototype might show wait time falling from
20 to 15 minutes against a 12-minute target, while household fare burden rises
from 4% to 6% against a no-increase guardrail. Show improved wait time, worsened
burden and target-specific statuses together. Label this descriptive change;
without a credible comparison design neither movement is attributed to the policy.

`/outcomes` should retain the labeled activity log in its own section and add a
separate policy-measurements section. Each metric card shows promise, baseline,
latest observation period, target/date, movement, uncertainty, freshness, owner,
caveats and source-history link. `/needs` links to these assessments while keeping
need-level conditions distinct. Missing data must occupy a visible card; do not
replace it with zero or quietly omit it. A stale prior observation stays visible
with its original date and a reason. What-if request values must never enter the
observed-results store. Leave `OUTCOMES_MODE` behavior unchanged during the spike.

## Verification and decision gates

| Acceptance criterion | Required positive and negative evidence before acceptance |
| --- | --- |
| Activity/effect separation | A saved reply increments only activity; it never creates enactment evidence or changes policy-effect status. Fictional, observed and scenario data retain visible labels. |
| Reviewable history | Reproduce baseline and current value from pinned snapshots; a corrected provider release retains the original baseline and earlier assessment. Missing hashes, schema changes and unit/geography mismatches block comparison. |
| Missing/stale data | Clock-controlled cases cover current data, exact freshness boundary, overdue release, no baseline, source failure, partial years and corrupt file. Preserve last good data without representing retrieval as a new measurement. |
| Mixed results | One improving and one worsening metric remain simultaneously visible; test higher-is-better and lower-is-better, zero denominators, unchanged values and uncertainty overlapping no change. |
| No unsupported causality | Before/after-only data always renders descriptive language with attribution unestablished. A missing/unreviewed comparison design cannot unlock a causal claim, including through request overrides. |

Require a data-governance review of source licensing/retention, provenance,
comparability, correction ownership, disclosure controls and who may register or
approve policy promises. Use aggregate public data; do not link journal identities
or voter identities to external individual records. Review source samples and
transformations against the actual archived inputs, not merely mocked fetches.

Then run `bun test`, `bun run typecheck` and `bun run test:routes` on the recovered
production-equivalent revision. Add browser evidence at narrow mobile widths,
keyboard-only navigation, 200% zoom and screen-reader reading order. Statuses need
text beyond color, accessible table/chart alternatives, descriptive source links,
visible focus and announced loading/error states. Verify histories, missing/stale
cards and mixed results without horizontal page overflow. These browser and
accessibility checks have not been performed by this analysis-only change.

Proceed with the remaining build only if reviewers can reproduce one real series,
confirm the promise and implementation evidence, and understand both tradeoffs
and uncertainty in the prototype. Otherwise record the source gap and revise scope.
A human must verify every done criterion; a spike/report or passing checks alone
is not completion of SHE-315.
