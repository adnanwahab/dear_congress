## Proposed user experience

Keep the current unavailable notice. Until all gates below pass, provide no email
field, notification permission prompt, waitlist, signup endpoint or pretend
subscribe control. An optional explanatory section can say: “Topical email digests
are not available. We are not collecting email addresses. Your browser watchlist
is separate and does not subscribe you.” A preview must be labeled a design/example
and must not present synthetic changes as checked events.

After readiness review, offer an optional entry beside the outcomes watchlist:
“Read-only topical digest — independently operated, non-binding beta; not a
government service.” Opening it records no enrollment. Show channel, explicitly
selected topics, weekly/monthly frequency, exactly what is retained and for how
long, and an unchecked consent control. Default delivery is off, with no topic
preselected from browser history, watchlist, location, ballots or journal text.
Submitting explicit consent may create a short-lived pending confirmation; only
an address-confirmation action activates delivery. State that pending status does
not mean subscribed. Do not create pending records from typing, blur or navigation.

A digest only reads reviewed public changes. Reading, receiving or unsubscribing
casts no vote, changes no proposal and conveys no political stance or membership.
Do not scrape contacts, upload address books, enroll existing users, auto-message
others, reward stances, add referral pressure or send advocacy/action campaigns.
A confirmation message is allowed only for a user-requested enrollment after
future implementation authorization; no messages are authorized by this review.

## Consent, unsubscribe and retention contract

These are proposed operational limits requiring owner review and executable
verification before collection, not a claim about an existing service or legal
compliance. No provider or retention contract has been selected here.

| State/data | Proposed rule |
| --- | --- |
| Before explicit submit | No newsletter storage, request payload, analytics event, log or provider call containing typed addresses or topics. No auto-save. |
| Pending | Store normalized address, explicit topic IDs/frequency, consent text version/time and hashed single-use confirmation token only. Expire/delete within 24 hours; no digest or reminder messages. Rate-limit without retaining raw addresses in logs. |
| Active | Store address, chosen preferences, consent/confirmation timestamps and version, subscription version and delivery cursor. Use only for this digest; no inferred political profile or joins to voting/account/browser identity. |
| Delivery artifacts | Keep minimal delivery result/event IDs for at most 7 days; avoid bodies, raw addresses and bearer tokens in logs. No open pixels or click tracking. |
| Unsubscribe | Every email has a working no-login unsubscribe path and supported one-click mechanism. A normal link opens a page with an explicit stop control so link scanners cannot accidentally change state; one-click requests use their dedicated validated endpoint. Stop delivery immediately and invalidate queued jobs/tokens. Recheck active status/version immediately before provider handoff. |
| Deletion | Delete address, topics and tokens from active systems/provider within 24 hours after unsubscribe, and backups within 30 days. Retain at most a non-identifying aggregate, never a topic/address mapping. A restore must reapply deletions before any sending resumes. |
| Already handed off | Explain that a message already accepted by the provider may arrive. Do not claim recall. Future batches and retries must remain stopped. |

Unsubscribe must be idempotent, scoped to the subscription, and available during
source outages without signing in or answering a survey. A fresh opt-in requires
new confirmation; it cannot revive canceled jobs. Topic/frequency changes must
be explicit and versioned. Expired or invalid tokens explain recovery without
revealing whether an address is enrolled. If unsubscribe, purge jobs, provider
cancellation or backup deletion cannot satisfy the published limits, collection
stays disabled. Document all processors and actual limits before presenting consent.
Avoid collecting extra identity just to enforce these rules.

## Source-checked content gate

Reuse the SHE-534 event contract rather than inventing a parallel feed. Every
included change must have source namespace, original repository/policy identity,
item kind, reviewed topic mapping, exact before/after revisions, dated evidence,
check time, source URLs and review provenance. Link to an immutable readable
comparison and the underlying sources. Check time is separate from source date;
a new hash or metadata refresh alone is not meaningful policy change. The first
observation is a baseline. Missing predecessors, conflicting evidence or unavailable
sources must be disclosed, not filled with synthetic text or labeled “no changes.”

Congress.gov metadata is a useful identity-bound foundation, but it does not supply
reviewed full-text revision comparisons or verified implementation results.
Synthetic outcomes, simulated sponsors and citizen text cannot enter the checked
newsletter lane. Unsupported kinds remain unavailable. Preserve corrections,
retractions, raw observations and revision histories. Freeze an explicit time
window/preference version, deduplicate by source identity/kind/revision pair and
make retries idempotent; failed delivery never advances a success cursor. An empty
digest describes coverage limits and makes no claim that government did nothing.

Preserve source repository identities, repository/item-kind isolation and PR
head/base binding. Never read, copy or mutate ballots, journals, receipts or private
records to build a newsletter. Treat explicit topic selection only as a delivery
instruction, not political preference, support, eligibility or merge authority.

## Acceptance still required

1. Implement and test the no-collection gate: mount, typing, abandon, denied
   consent and existing follows/browser topic choices produce no subscription
   records, address-bearing logs, provider calls or inferred preferences.
2. Verify explicit consent, confirmation/expiry, no prechecked controls, duplicate
   enrollment, abuse limits, changed topics/frequency and failed storage/provider
   behavior. Consent copy must match actual retention and processors.
3. Exercise unsubscribe races with queued sends/retries, in-flight handoff,
   replay/expired tokens, repeated stop requests, source outages, deletion from
   provider/storage/backups and safe restore. Demonstrate deadlines with a clock.
4. Review real sourced examples and exact revision links; test cross-repository,
   item-kind and PR-revision collisions, stale/missing/conflicting sources,
   corrections, empty/partial coverage and duplicate events. Fixtures never
   establish a checked real event or inflate participation.
5. Human review at desktop/mobile, keyboard, screen reader and 200% zoom must
   confirm people understand optionality, read-only scope, non-governmental
   status, source limits, retention and how to stop. No UI was added or tested here.
6. Evaluate usefulness and comprehension through optional non-sensitive feedback;
   measure whether readers can locate sources and unsubscribe. Do not treat
   deliveries, opens, sharing or signup counts as meaningful participation.
