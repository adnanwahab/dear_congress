# Ten exploratory reform concepts for informed public review

**These are hypothetical discussion drafts — not legal advice, not official bills, not claims of universal support, and not evidence that a law needs change in a particular jurisdiction.** Before publication as an active consultation, research current law, identify authority and affected populations, estimate fiscal/administrative impact, solicit multiple perspectives and check civil rights protections. Keep sponsorship status `none` unless verified.

Each concept is meant to be imported into a **review-only** proposal queue. Do not insert it into a live official vote or report it as already introduced.

## 1. Public Response Clock

**Goal:** make official responses to qualifying public petitions more trackable.

**Illustrative policy mechanism:** require agencies within a defined jurisdiction to acknowledge a qualifying petition and issue a reasoned public disposition within a set deadline, subject to capacity, legal exemptions and appeal rules. The disposition may be "needs more information" or "outside agency authority." **Jurisdiction and threshold must be defined before drafting a real bill.**

**Trade-offs:** agency capacity, coordinated spam, administrative law timelines, petitions concerning personal privacy. **Metrics:** median acknowledgment days, reasoned-response rate, agency workload and appeal outcomes.

## 2. Readable Laws and Versioned Legislative Text

**Goal:** make legal changes understandable and traceable.

**Mechanism:** require official publication of versioned and machine-readable legal text with human-reviewed summaries, effective dates, history and source links; clarify that official text remains controlling. **Trade-offs:** authoritative source maintenance, translation and summary errors, implementation cost. **Metrics:** update lag, correction rate, public comprehension, machine readability.

## 3. Right to Human Review of Consequential Automated Decisions

**Goal:** ensure contestability for specified significant automated government determinations.

**Mechanism:** mandate notice, explanation to the extent permitted by law, accessible human reconsideration, appeal time limits and recordkeeping when an automated system materially affects eligibility or benefits. **Trade-offs:** personnel needs, privacy/security exceptions, administrative delay, legal harmonization. **Metrics:** appeal turnaround, reversal rate, errors and access disparities.

## 4. Government Decision Receipt

**Goal:** reduce unexplained government process uncertainty.

**Mechanism:** send applicants for services, licenses and benefits a status receipt identifying the decision, rationale categories, responsible office, next step and appeal path, with secure delivery. **Trade-offs:** accessibility, data privacy, legacy systems, special cases. **Metrics:** appeals understood, support contacts, processing duration, errors corrected.

## 5. One Form Public Services

**Goal:** reduce redundant form filling where law permits.

**Mechanism:** let people consent to reuse selected verified fields across eligible agencies, with purpose limits, error correction, auditable access and a manual alternative. **Trade-offs:** data minimization, security, exclusion of people lacking IDs, interoperability. **Metrics:** completion time, error rate, consent reversal and accessibility outcomes.

## 6. Open Public Spending and Delivery Milestones

**Goal:** increase visibility into public contracts and project results.

**Mechanism:** publish machine-readable project budgets, contract changes, milestones, responsible entities, progress and independently specified exceptions. **Trade-offs:** sensitive procurement information, business confidentiality, reporting cost, data quality. **Metrics:** update timeliness, completeness, audit findings, public usage.

## 7. Small Business Permit Clock

**Goal:** make permit pathways predictable.

**Mechanism:** publish standard checklists, application states and review deadlines, with escalation for delays; preserve substantive inspections and legal environmental/safety review. **Trade-offs:** staffing, application complexity, procedural fairness. **Metrics:** median approval times, rework, safety compliance, successful appeals.

## 8. Reversible Policy Pilot Framework

**Goal:** evaluate some policies on evidence before wider implementation.

**Mechanism:** authorize bounded pilot programs with public hypotheses, budget caps, duration, independent evaluation, equity protections, consent where applicable and explicit stop rules. **Trade-offs:** unequal access, external validity, administrative costs, rights constraints. **Metrics:** preregistration completeness, real-world outcomes, adverse events, published evaluations.

## 9. Care Access Transparency

**Goal:** report whether people can actually obtain needed appointments.

**Mechanism:** define standardized, privacy-preserving measures of provider-directory accuracy and appointment waiting times for covered services; require data quality and appeals. **Trade-offs:** burden on clinics, gaming, geography, accessibility and privacy. **Metrics:** validated directory accuracy, median waits, no-show rates, rural access.

## 10. Neighborhood Infrastructure Accountability

**Goal:** make drainage, sidewalks, crossings and similar priorities inspectable.

**Mechanism:** publish prioritized projects with criteria, estimated risks, budget, schedule, accessible map and verified milestones. **Trade-offs:** geographic equity, differing urgency measures, small-neighborhood privacy, infrastructure risk disclosures. **Metrics:** milestones, change orders, hazards mitigated, safety outcomes.

## Editorial review requirements

- Conduct a current-law and jurisdiction check. A measure already enacted in part does not justify a claim of novelty.
- Compare at least one alternative and a no-change baseline.
- State who benefits, who bears cost, credible risks and uncertain impacts without partisan framing.
- Separate binding legal text, administrative rules and voluntary proposals.
- Require human legal/editorial approval before text becomes a named draft bill or appears as officially endorsed.
- Only include sources that have been verified; until then use `research_needed` placeholders instead of invented references or amounts.
