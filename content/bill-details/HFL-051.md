# Third Places for Everyone Act

Discussion draft supplement, version 1. Illustrative city legislation for legal, fiscal and evidence review. This supplement does not replace the six-field catalog entry or authorize spending.

## Statutory text

### Section 1. Purpose and authority

The proposed purpose is to improve access to welcoming noncommercial gathering spaces. City counsel must verify local authority, constitutional requirements and compatibility with existing facilities before introduction.

### Section 2. Accessible community spaces

Subject to a separately approved appropriation, the city would establish a transparent program supporting recreation centers, community halls and other accessible noncommercial gathering spaces. Participation would not require a purchase, political affiliation or religious observance.

### Section 3. Operating safeguards

Applicants would publish accessibility plans, hours, staffing, maintenance and safeguarding arrangements. Selection would use public, viewpoint-neutral criteria and conflict-of-interest disclosures. Residents would have an accessible complaint and appeal route. Collect only the minimum information needed to operate safely; attendance reporting should be aggregated without identifying visitors.

### Section 4. Oversight and review

The city would publish program criteria and aggregate results, invite resident feedback and independently review continued funding. No individual right to funding or appropriation is established by this discussion draft.

## Proposed funding scenarios

- Reuse existing facilities: compare extended hours and staffing against current capacity and maintenance needs.
- Competitive operating grants: assess recurring appropriations, equitable access and transparent award conditions.
- Limited pilot: compare a time-limited neighborhood pilot with broader delivery before committing recurring funds.

All scenarios require independent fiscal assessment. Fiscal estimate: Unknown until assessed. No dollar amount, savings or revenue source has been validated.

## Pro arguments

- Free gathering spaces could reduce barriers to meeting neighbors and participating in community life.
- Reusing existing facilities could improve access without new construction, subject to local capacity review.

## Con arguments and objections

- Operating and safeguarding obligations may create substantial recurring costs.
- Low attendance, exclusionary practices or unequal geographic coverage could undermine the purpose.
- Expanded hours could displace existing activities or place unsustainable demands on staff.

## Evidence plan

Establish a baseline of free gathering-space access, opening hours and accessibility by neighborhood. Consult residents, including disabled residents and people who do not attend existing programs. Predefine access, repeat attendance, safety and perceived belonging measures with voluntary privacy-preserving collection. Compare pilot and baseline conditions, publish uncertainty and adverse effects, and commission independent review before expansion. No effectiveness finding is asserted.
